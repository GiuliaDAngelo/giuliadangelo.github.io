Overview

Motivated by the development of efficient, brain-inspired perception and control systems, this course introduces neuromorphic sensing and neuromorphic computing for robotics and computer vision. The emphasis is on event-based vision and spiking neural networks (SNNs) for vision-based tasks, and on how they differ from classical artificial neural networks. The final part of the course gives an overview of SNNs: their key computational principles, possible implementations, and applications.

You will gain a general overview of event-based sensing and neuromorphic computing: core components, processing approaches, and learning rules; together with insight into real-world robotic applications, including the neuromorphic iCub and developments from companies such as IBM, BrainChip, SynSense, Innatera, iNivation, Neuronova, Neuomorphic Intel, etc. Comparisons with standard frame-based approaches are drawn where useful, but the course is not meant as a full introduction to conventional methods.

Lectures

  1. Neuromorphic sensing and computing — the work of Carver Mead, Misha Mahowald and Rodney Douglas at Caltech, and the path to Infineon, iniVation and Prophesee; motivation, history, and relevance.
  2. Neuromorphic sensing: the retina — contrast sensitivity, the Weber–Fechner law, Mach bands, lateral inhibition, gain modulation mechanisms.
  3. Neuromorphic sensing: the human visual system — retinotopic mapping, retino-cortical processing, cortical magnification, cortical processing (LGN, V1, V2).
  4. Event-based cameras: visual odometry — motion estimation, contrast maximisation, SLAM; comparison with classical approaches.
  5. Event-based cameras: navigation and motion detection — Reichardt detectors and sEMD.
  6. Event-based cameras: stereopsis and depth perception — Osswald, Marr, Poggio, Mahowald.
  7. Event-driven sensing beyond vision — touch, the event-based cochlea, olfactory circuits and their applications.
  8. Spiking neural networks — overview and comparison with classical ANNs, analog vs digital, spike detection and analysis (mean firing rate, inter-spike interval), temporal coding, and learning rules.

Labs

Lab material lives in the CTU-EDNeuromorphic GitHub repository.

  • Event-based visualisation and event representation methods
  • Event-based Object Motion Sensitivity (OMS) and visual attention
  • Journal club — recent literature and open problems
  • Project brainstorming based on the lectures and journal club (a list of possible projects is provided)
  • Final session: project and demo presentations to the class

Who it's for

A compulsory elective in the Computer Vision specialisation of the Open Informatics master's programme (recommended in the 2nd semester), and open to other students as an elective. The class is kept small — ideally 15 to 25 participants.

Basic programming (Python) and familiarity with linear algebra and signals are helpful. No prior neuroscience background is required.
